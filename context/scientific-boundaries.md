# Scientific and Communication Boundaries

## Supported Statements

- Signal quality and recording integrity
- Sensor-level posterior EEG patterns
- PSD and frequency-band power
- Posterior Alpha power and peak-frequency dynamics
- Sparse posterior Alpha asymmetry
- Relative within-recording Visual Cognitive Load labels
- Possible acquisition problems such as movement, muscle activity, line noise,
  unstable contact, or malformed input rows

## Unsupported Statements

- Medical diagnosis, treatment, or clinical decision-making
- Mental-health, emotion, deception, or personality inference
- Fatigue or attention diagnosis from a single feature or ratio
- Absolute or cross-participant Visual Cognitive Load diagnosis
- Cortical source localization from this sparse montage
- Causal conclusions from uncontrolled comparisons
- Population claims from one participant or one recording

## Low-Quality Interpretation

Continue interpreting a completed workflow when quality is low, but:

1. Put the quality warning near the beginning.
2. Quote the relevant retention rate, rejected segments, bad channels, or
   excluded windows.
3. Explain which conclusions remain descriptive and which become uncertain.
4. Avoid filling gaps by interpolation or speculation.
5. Recommend checking electrode contact, headset placement, participant
   movement, muscle tension, cable stability, nearby electrical sources, and
   protocol timing when relevant.
